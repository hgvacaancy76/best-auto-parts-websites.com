Import taxes in the United Kingdom

Goods sent to Great Britain are charged import VAT on their value. Customs Duty applies to excise goods and to goods worth more than £135, and the rate depends on the type of goods. HMRC published a policy paper on 13 July 2026 that removes the £135 relief, and the current rule applies until the change takes effect, so check GOV.UK. The parcel or courier company tells you if you need to pay VAT or duty, and it may charge a handling fee.

Before you order

  1. Enter a UK address at checkout to confirm the store ships there.

  2. Check whether VAT is added at checkout or collected on delivery.

  3. Read return terms. Autodoc allows 14 days from delivery, and Cars245 needs approval within 14 days of receipt, and the buyer pays return shipping at Cars245.

  4. Match the part by OEM number and VIN before paying.

  5. Keep the invoice, because customs may ask for the value of the parts.

If a store does not ship to the United Kingdom

If a store does not ship to the United Kingdom, a package forwarder can receive the parcel at a United States address and send it on. MyUS says it offers both US and UK shipping addresses. Forwarders add fees and customs steps, and some stores restrict them. Read the forwarders guide before choosing one.

Frequently asked questions

Which stores deliver car parts to the UK?

Autodoc lists Great Britain on its delivery page. Cars245 states worldwide shipping, and RockAuto says it ships everywhere unless prohibited by law. O'Reilly Auto Parts and CarParts.com state that they ship only within the United States. Check the table above for each store's status.

Do I pay VAT on car parts from abroad?

Yes, GOV.UK says import VAT is charged on the value of goods sent to Great Britain, and the seller or the carrier may collect it. Customs Duty applies to excise goods and to goods worth more than £135. Confirm the rule on GOV.UK before ordering.

Is the low value relief going away?

Yes, it is being removed. HMRC published a policy paper on 13 July 2026 confirming that the £135 relief for Customs Duty will be removed. Baker McKenzie reports that removal is expected by October 2028 at the latest. The current rule applies until then, so check GOV.UK before you order.
