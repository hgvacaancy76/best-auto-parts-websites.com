Import Duties on Car Parts Ordered From Abroad

By Editorial team/Editorial policy/Last updated: {{updated}}

Yes, a car part ordered from another country can attract import duty, import tax or both. What you pay depends on your country, the value of the parcel, the type of part and whether the store collects tax at checkout. The rules below were checked on 18 November 2026 against official pages. They change often, so confirm the current rule before you order.

Import duty rules by country

United States

The United States has suspended the $800 de minimis exemption for postal and courier shipments, so low-value parcels are no longer exempt from duty. For mail, a Federal Register rule of June 24, 2026 keeps the suspension in place and adds a new postal informal entry process effective July 24, 2026.

Checked 18 November 2026

United Kingdom

Goods sent to Great Britain are charged import VAT. Customs Duty applies to excise goods and to goods worth more than £135. HMRC published a policy paper on 13 July 2026 that removes the £135 relief, with removal expected by October 2028 at the latest according to Baker McKenzie, so check GOV.UK before ordering.

Checked 18 November 2026

Canada

The Canada Border Services Agency says an item worth CAN$20 or less sent by mail carries no duty or tax. Above CAN$20 you pay the applicable duty, GST or HST and any PST on the item's full value.

Checked 18 November 2026

Australia

GST applies at the point of sale when the overseas supplier is above the registration threshold. Customs duty and clearance charges may apply at the border. For goods with a customs value above A$1,000, GST is payable at the border.

Checked 18 November 2026

Israel

Israel changed its personal import thresholds several times between December 2025 and February 2026, and sources differ. A DHL notice says the VAT exemption threshold fell from US$150 to US$130 on 25 February 2026. Purchase tax can also apply.

Checked 18 November 2026

How to estimate landed cost

The landed cost is the part price, plus shipping and insurance, plus customs duty and import tax. In Australia, the tax office says shipping and insurance costs are part of the price on which GST is calculated. Some stores also collect brokerage fees and carrier charges when you place the order. Add every item before comparing stores, because a lower part price can lose to higher shipping and duties. Keep the full total for each store, and compare totals, not part prices.

Before you order

  1. Check whether the store collects tax and duties at checkout. RockAuto says it collects named taxes such as GST, HST or VAT, plus duties and brokerage fees, when the order is placed.

  2. Check who pays on delivery. FCP Euro says its carriers do not offer prepaid duties, so the carrier determines them at delivery.

  3. Check return costs. RockAuto says duties cannot be refunded if you return a product.

  4. Keep the commercial invoice. RockAuto sends one when the warehouse and the delivery address are in different countries.

  5. Read your government's page for the rule that applies to your parcel.

Part classification and HS codes

Customs authorities set duty by what the item is, using a tariff code, so different parts can carry different rates. GOV.UK says the rate depends on the type of goods and points to the Trade Tariff service for lookups. Ask the store or the carrier which code appears on the invoice, then compare it with your country's tariff before you order.

Frequently asked questions

Do I pay import duties on car parts bought abroad?

Often yes. It depends on your country, the value of the parcel and how the store collects tax. Some stores collect at checkout, while others leave it to the carrier at delivery. Read the market notes above and the store's own help page before you pay for the order.

Are import duties refunded when I return a part?

RockAuto states that duties cannot be refunded if you return a product. Other stores publish different terms or none. Read the return policy before ordering, and ask whether return shipping is also charged to you, because the buyer pays it at several stores.

Why do import rules keep changing?

Governments update low-value import rules often. The United States suspended its $800 de minimis exemption for postal and courier shipments, the United Kingdom confirmed the removal of its £135 relief, and Israel changed its thresholds more than once. Rely on dated government pages instead of old articles.
